##### **A new positioning for Absa** 

Throughout our brand journey, Absa has focused on creating a brand that is authentically African. A bank for the people, with its roots and an outlook that is firmly of this soil. We took inspiration from the people and the landscapes – the things that are inherent parts of our identity. 

As we embark on a new chapter in the Absa story, we’re embracing a narrative that is focused on people and their stories. By placing ourselves as a supporting character in their lives, we aim to play a meaningful role in their potential to live a better life. To them, we say, “Your story matters”. And this is our new positioning. 

We want to put our customers and clients first in everything we do – it’s their story and we are there to support them empathetically, by offering financial solutions that will help them achieve their goals. 

The new positioning also affects how we show up as a brand from a visual identity perspective. We have condensed our visual identity, to mark the change and make it more simplified and distinctive. Please take careful consideration of the following guidelines as there have been important changes from previous versions. 

###### **The story of our colours** 

As a brand born of Africa, we embrace the profound essence of red. It signifies our deep-rooted connection with Africa. Just as red courses through the veins of this vibrant continent, it pulses through its people, symbolising passion, courage and the unyielding spirit that defines us. And it’s this passion that inspired our colour palette. 

Spearheaded by Passion red, a colour that’s the very embodiment of the African spirit, to a range of secondary red hues and accent colours that help bring this fiery essence to life. It’s these vibrant colours that inspired our purpose to passionately empower Africa and give back to its people. 

By using this colour palette every day, we live out our values and put our purpose into action.  And it’s through our use of this vibrant colour that we reflect on our past, nod our heads to today and paint a picture of tomorrow.

###### **This is what red feels like – gradients** 

Using gradients that feature Passion red blending into a secondary colour can be achieved while still maintaining a predominantly red look. Gradients always start from bottom left to top right (at a 45 degree angle) and always start with Passion. The gradient should be 70% Passion leading to 30% of the second colour. 

Gradients may only be used for social media, digital banners, powerpoint presentations and emailers. Gradients can also be used for QTV screens with iconography and occasionally for videos. 

The only time gradients can be used for print material is for divider pages (using the gestures patterns), guidebooks and pitch books.

###### **Contrast variations** 

The following pages show the recommended high-, mediumand low-contrast colour variations for RGB (digital) applications, as the document is set in RGB. 

RGB applications include examples such as emailers, digital banners, social media posts, videos, etc. 

CMYK applications include examples such as posters, billboards, pamphlets, brochures, business cards, etc. 

Please avoid using the same colour text as the background. 

If a high impact is intended, such as an attention-grabbing billboard headline, high-contrast colours should be used. High-contrast colours are equally appropriate where high-yield is required, such as in the body copy of a report or when displaying key information. 

Medium-contrast combinations can be used where a subtle or low-impact effect is intended. Examples of appropriate medium-contrast applications include Wealth product branding, wallpaper artwork and backgrounds. Low-contrast combinations can be used on tonal applications that are more aesthetic than informative. 

Please note that due to varying monitor and printer settings, colour combinations should always be checked using accurate and dependable colour proofing systems before approving artwork for production.

- Do the colours in the visual work together to convey a feeling of red with a touch of accent? This applies to layouts and 

- graphics (illustrations and bespoke patterns). **_See p35-49_** 

- Do the chosen colour combinations align with the application ratio principle? Always remember that we need to balance the 

   - chosen colours to ensure that we show up as a red brand, as opposed to not being red enough. **_See p12_**

###### Gradients 

Our organisational purpose is “Empowering Africa’s tomorrow, together ... one story at a time” 

As a brand, it is important for us to deliver value to you, meet your needs and make your life easier. We developed this checklist as a quick and easy way for you to check whether you applied the most important principles found in the this guide correctly. Always use this checklist before flighting your marketing, communications and other creative collateral. 

- Does the applied gradient consist predominantly of red? No gradient should exclude the use of Passion. **_See p14-17_** 

- Does the applied tricolour gradient move from light to dark? Avoid jumping between light and dark colours, rather allow colours 

- to gradually flow from a light to dark spectrum. **_See p17_** 

- Are you using the appropriate gradient for your application? Gradients using Energy and Uplift will result in a more vibrant look, 

- whereas gradients using Enrich and Inspire will result in a more sophisticated look. **_See p15_** 

- Check that gradients are only applied to backgrounds. **_See p14_**
